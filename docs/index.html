What it does

Large language models can sound confident and still be wrong. In a methods course, a wrong explanation of a p-value or an ANOVA harms learning, so the tutor is built around grounding and transparency.

Grounded in the courseAnswers come first from 12 ERM lecture units, from experimental design to ANOVA, regression and bias in research.
Shows its sourceEvery answer says whether it comes from the course materials (and which unit) or from general research-methods knowledge.
📘 Course Materials: 7. T-test and ANNOVA test
Behaves like a tutorA short empathetic reflection, the explanation, and a follow-up question that keeps the student thinking, in one natural reply.
Stays on topic, stays shortOff-topic questions are politely declined, and each answer shows its word count to keep replies focused.

How it works

Retrieval-Augmented Generation: retrieve the relevant course passages first, then let the model answer from them.

  1. Index the courseLecture PDFs are parsed with Markitdown, split into chunks and embedded with sentence-transformers into ChromaDB.
  2. RetrieveFor each question, the 4 most relevant and diverse chunks are selected (maximal marginal relevance).
  3. GenerateGPT-4o answers with a tutor prompt: course materials first, general ERM knowledge second, decline if off-topic.
  4. PresentA Streamlit chat shows the answer, its source and its length.

Designed in four iterations

A design-based research approach: each version was reviewed and refined for usability, visual identity and pedagogical orientation.

Limitations and next steps

  • 20 questions and two raters; inter-rater reliability not reported; ratings not blind to the system; likely ceiling effects.
  • No study with students yet. Learning gains, engagement and trust are the next questions to test.
  • The knowledge base is limited to the course materials; other architectures (fine-tuning, hybrid retrieval) were not compared.
